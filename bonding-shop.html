How it really behaves

  • Without a VPS, true single-connection bonding via MPTCP only works when the far-end server also speaks MPTCP. Most websites don't.
  • Otherwise the PC load-balances separate connections across the lines: many users and downloads together get the combined speed, while one single download or stream stays on one line.
  • Services at the shop can't be reached from the internet without a VPS or tunnel.

Router-based bonding

Same mechanism, different firmware. Five lines into one router, one logical link out, every device behind it benefits.

B · Peplink + SpeedFusion bonding

How router bonding works

  • The combining happens inside the router's firmware at the transport/tunnel layer: OpenMPTCProuter spreads MPTCP subflows across the lines, and Peplink SpeedFusion splits packets of one tunnel across the lines. It is not done by IP routing tables.
  • Every LAN device (phones, PCs, POS) gets the combined speed without supporting MPTCP or anything special itself.
  • Both need a bonding endpoint on the far side to put the traffic back together into one connection for a single flow: OpenMPTCProuter pairs with a small VPS running its server part; Peplink SpeedFusion pairs with SpeedFusion Cloud, a FusionHub (virtual appliance on a VPS/cloud server) or another Peplink router.
  • Without that far-end endpoint, the router can only load-balance separate connections (per session), just like the PC option above.
